## ENTRY & PAYMENT FLAGS

**VISA — the routing works (verify closer to travel):**
- US citizens qualify for China's **240-hour (10-day) visa-free transit** (TWOV) — no visa needed — when transiting to a *different* country/region than the one arrived from. **Hong Kong counts as a "third region"** relative to mainland China, so **HK → Shanghai → Tokyo qualifies** (origin HK ≠ destination Japan).
- Shanghai (both PVG and SHA Hongqiao airports) is among the 65 eligible entry ports; since the Dec 2024 expansion travelers may move across provinces during the window. 3 nights uses ~72 of 240 hours — huge margin.
- Requirements: passport valid 3+ months, **confirmed onward ticket to Tokyo** (must show at check-in in HK and at Shanghai immigration), completed arrival card, use the dedicated TWOV immigration lane (can take 30–60 min of paperwork on arrival — budget for it).
- **CRITICAL FLAGS**: (1) Policy could change by May 2027 — re-verify ~3 months out at the Chinese embassy/NIA site. (2) Airlines in HK sometimes push back on visa-less boarding — carry a printout of the 240-hour policy and the Tokyo itinerary. (3) All four family members must qualify individually (kids fine — same rules). (4) Alternative if nervous: 10-year US tourist visa (~$185/person, but TWOV is free and sufficient).

**PAYMENT:** China is functionally cashless. Before arrival: install **Alipay** (primary) and **WeChat Pay** (backup), link Visa/Mastercard, complete passport verification in-app. Transactions under ¥200 have 0% foreign-card fee; 3% above that. Carry ¥500–1,000 cash as backup (some vendors struggle to make change). Foreign cards work at hotels/Disney/upscale restaurants only. **Set up Alipay while still in the US/HK.**

**CONNECTIVITY:** Google, Gmail, Maps, Instagram, WhatsApp are blocked by the Great Firewall. **A travel eSIM with roaming data (Airalo, Nomad, Saily, Holafly) routes through the home carrier and bypasses the Firewall entirely — no VPN needed.** Must install **before** landing. VPNs are unreliable (2026 crackdown). Apps to install pre-trip: Alipay, WeChat, **Shanghai Disney Resort official app**, Didi (rideshare — has English mode, inside Alipay too), Metro Man or Apple Maps (works in China), translation app with offline Chinese pack.

## LODGING VERDICT

**Recommendation: SPLIT-LEAN → single base in the city center (People's Square / Bund corridor), NOT Disneyland-area, NOT a split.**

- With only 3 nights, a hotel split burns half a day packing/moving. Skip it.
- Metro Line 11 runs direct to Disney Resort station (~45–60 min from People's Square with one change at Oriental Sports Center, ~¥6; taxi/Didi 45–60 min, ~¥150–200). Doable as two day-trips.
- City-center base wins because: the city day and both evenings (Bund at night is the single best free thing in Shanghai) need zero extra transit; dinner options are 100× better; PVG departure Sunday is straightforward (Maglev or Didi).
- Tradeoff honestly stated: Disney-area base saves ~2 hrs commuting across the 2 park days and makes rope-drop trivial, but strands the family for the city day and all dinners, and the on-property hotels (Shanghai Disneyland Hotel ~¥2,400+/night) are the weakest value in the trip. Toy Story Hotel (~¥1,000) is the budget on-property option if the parent agent overrides.
- Sweet spot hotels (mid-range, family rooms, near Line 11/2/10 interchange): Jing'an or People's Square international chains (e.g., Le Royal Méridien People's Square, Fairmont Peace Hotel = splurge, Holiday Inn Express Zhabei = value). Last Line 11 train back from Disney ~10:30 PM — fine for post-fireworks.

## DISNEY 2-DAY PLAN

**Tickets & systems (verify pricing 2027):**
- **Real-name ticketing**: buy date-specific tickets in advance (official app/site, Trip.com, Klook); enter each traveler's **passport name + number**; bring the physical passports to the gate. All guests on one order enter together. No separate park reservation beyond the dated ticket itself.
- 1-day ticket ~¥475–719 depending on tier; 2-day tickets get a small discount. Late May weekday = mid tier.
- **Early Park Entry Pass** (~¥159–199/person): enter 1 hr before official open. Worth it on the Saturday; optional Friday.
- **Disney Premier Access** (paid line-skip): singles ¥90–200/ride; bundles ~¥890 (6-ride) to ¥1,220–1,400 (8-ride) ≈ $125–195/person. **Verdict: buy the bundle for Saturday only** (weekend waits hit 120–150 min on Zootopia/TRON/Soaring); Friday rope-drop + single DPA for one headliner is usually enough. Bundles open ~10 days out and sell out on peak days — buy early via the app.
- **Standby Pass**: on crowded days some attractions (historically Zootopia) switch to free timed-entry passes claimed in the app — check the app at park open; if Zootopia is on Standby Pass, grab it immediately at rope drop.
- **Crowds May 27–30, 2027**: **Dragon Boat Festival 2027 is June 9** — safely outside the window, no holiday surge. Expect moderate weekday crowds Fri, heavy Sat (Saturday is the busiest day; ~31 min average waits vs ~27 midweek — headliners far worse).

**Must-rides (unique to Shanghai):**
1. **Zootopia: Hot Pursuit** — world's only Zootopia land (opened 2023); trackless dark ride; longest waits in park.
2. **TRON Lightcycle Power Run** — the original, and faster/more intense than Florida's; ride it twice, once at night.
3. **Pirates of the Caribbean: Battle for the Sunken Treasure** — widely called the best dark ride Disney has built anywhere; magnetic boats, massive screens. Do not skip; waits are moderate because capacity is huge.
4. **Soaring Over the Horizon** — Shanghai's version of Soarin'; long waits, DPA candidate.
5. **Camp Discovery Challenge Trails** — harnessed ropes course over Adventure Isle; min height 106 cm (both girls clear it easily), under-122 cm needs an accompanying adult. Unique to Shanghai, kids adore it, low waits — do mid-afternoon.
6. **Enchanted Storybook Castle** — largest Disney castle; has a walkthrough attraction, boat ride beneath it (Voyage to the Crystal Grotto), and the nightly projection/fireworks show (ILLUMINATE).
7. Peter Pan's Flight (upgraded version), Roaring Rapids (you WILL get soaked — ponchos).

**Rope-drop plan (both days):** arrive 60–75 min before official open (gates/security are slow — bag X-ray for every guest). With Early Entry, head straight to **Zootopia** (or grab its Standby Pass), then **TRON**, then **Soaring**, then Pirates before lunch. Afternoon: Camp Discovery, castle, shows. Night: TRON re-ride, castle show.

**Dining/snacks in park:** the famous **hoisin-glazed turkey leg** (2-per-person limit, longest snack line in the park), Mickey-shaped pretzels (~¥30), Mickey pizza at Pinocchio Village Kitchen (~¥85), surprisingly good xiaolongbao in-park, wild popcorn flavors (steak!) with collectible buckets. Table-service worth it once: **Lumière's Kitchen** or Royal Banquet Hall (character meal, book via app). Note: "Peppercorn Kitchen" could not be confirmed as a current SDL venue — likely confusion with Spiced Peppercorn-style counter service; flag for the app check.

**Community wisdom:** queue culture is real — guard your place politely, personal space is smaller; locals sprint at rope drop (keep kids' hands held); bring a portable battery (the app drains phones); umbrellas double as parasols; the park is *cheap* by US Disney standards for food; **mistake to avoid**: arriving at official open (you're 45 min behind), skipping Pirates because "we have it at home" (you don't — this one is different), and underestimating security-line time. **Overrated**: Dumbo/carousel-tier rides (skip; identical everywhere), Once Upon a Time storybook walkthrough if time-pressed.

## CITY DAY (geographically clustered, ~compressed into Thu PM + evening, or full Sat)

**Core loop (all within one Metro Line 10/2 corridor):**
- **Morning**: **Yu Garden + Yuyuan Bazaar** (classical Ming garden, ~¥30–40; go at open 8:30–9 AM before tour groups; bazaar = dumplings + souvenir quests).
- **Midday**: walk/short Didi to the **Bund** promenade — skyline photo op; then cross to Pudong via Metro Line 2 (skip the kitschy Sightseeing Tunnel — verify it's even open).
- **Afternoon**: **Shanghai Tower** — but do it via **Duoyun Books, the world's highest bookstore, on the 52nd floor**: reservation is free, views over Lujiazui/Huangpu nearly match the paid observation deck (118F, ~¥180/adult), and for two reader daughters a bookstore in the clouds beats an observation deck. Do the official deck only if the day is crystal clear.
- **Late afternoon (pick one)**: **Shanghai Science & Technology Museum** (interactive, Metro Line 2, kids love robotics/space halls) — the **Shanghai Astronomy Museum** is world-class (largest planetarium on Earth) but 2 hrs away in Lingang; **not feasible** on this trip — flag as "next time."
- **Evening**: **French Concession** stroll (Wukang Road/Anfu Road, plane trees, gelato, boutiques) → dinner → **Bund at night** (lights on until 10 PM) or Huangpu River night cruise (~¥120, 50 min — great kid finale).

**2 offbeat picks:**
1. **Duoyun Books (Shanghai Tower 52F)** — see above; tailor-made for this family.
2. **Propaganda Poster Art Centre** (French Concession basement museum, ~¥25) — quirky, genuinely fascinating Cold-War poster collection; 13-year-old history-curious kids do well here. Alternate: **Shanghai Postal Museum** (free, rooftop view) or **M50 art district**.

## LOGISTICS

- **HKG→Shanghai**: fly (2.5–3 hr; Cathay, China Eastern, Juneyao, Spring). **Prefer landing at SHA Hongqiao if available** — 30 min from People's Square vs PVG's 60–90 min; both airports are 240-hr TWOV ports. High-speed rail from West Kowloon exists (8–8.5 hr) — not worth it here.
- **PVG arrival (if PVG)**: TWOV lane → **Maglev to Longyang Road** (¥50/adult, 8 min, the kid-delight — show same-day boarding pass for ¥40) → Metro Line 2 or Didi to hotel.
- **Metro**: cheap (¥3–9), clean, English signage; use Alipay's transport QR code — no ticket machines needed. Security bag-scan at every station.
- **Didi**: the Uber of China; English app; pay via linked card/Alipay; ~¥30–50 for most city hops; ~¥150–200 city↔Disney.
- **To Disneyland**: Metro Line 11 to Disney Resort station (~45–60 min from People's Sq), 5–10 min walk via Disneytown; last return train ~10:30 PM. Didi back after fireworks if melting down.
- **Weather late May**: 25–29 °C days (can spike to ~33 °C), 18 °C nights, humid (~70%+), ~15 rain-days in May but mostly drizzle/short showers. Pack: breathable layers, ponchos (Roaring Rapids + drizzle), sunscreen, portable fans.
- **Etiquette**: queue-guarding, louder public spaces than US norm, tipping is NOT done anywhere, hot water is the default drink, bring tissues (some restrooms lack paper), squat toilets common outside malls/hotels (Disney has Western).
- **PVG→Tokyo May 30**: dozens of daily nonstops (China Eastern, JAL, ANA, Spring, Peach; ~3 hr). Arrive PVG 3 hrs early — exit immigration for TWOV is routine but lines are long Sunday mornings. Maglev starts ~6:45 AM (fits any flight after ~9:30 AM); Didi to PVG ~¥170, 45–60 min from downtown.
